# Novelty audit: unresolved, with one established overlap

**Current verdict:** novelty has not been demonstrated. Research-network access
prevents a defensible negative search for the full proposed combination.

| Proposed element | Evidence available in this run | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-body antibody PBPK + two-pore transport + FcRn + lymph | Explicit in retrieved OSP reports | Established; cannot claim this combination as new |
| Tissue/gut prediction and FcRn knockout evaluation | OSP 7E3 report and numerical snapshot | Established in mice; reuse is not independent human validation |
| Adalimumab + intestinal disease physiology | Mandatory citations and dedicated searches blocked | Prior-art status unresolved |
| TNF binding/turnover in disease-specific antibody PBPK | Primary-source search blocked | Prior-art status unresolved |
| Single-cell information + FcRn/TNF + biopsy validation | TAURUS article/metadata unavailable | Prior-art and functional mapping unresolved |
| Bayesian inference for the full combination | Search blocked | Method choice alone is not a novelty claim |

Primary hypothesis remains a **candidate**: can independently parameterised
intestinal disease physiology improve prediction of anti-TNF tissue concentrations
over a matched PBPK reference? Test it with a tissue cohort withheld from parameter
selection and fitting. Predefine drug, bowel segment, activity strata, analyte,
sampling window, and comparison metrics after establishing what the source data
actually measure, before examining prediction errors.

The possible contribution is a falsifiable transfer test with transparent
physiology provenance and an assay-consistent observation model. Combining more
mechanisms, fitting tissue data better in-sample, or including single-cell
covariates does not by itself answer this hypothesis.

Before claiming a gap, screen S01–S12 completely, verify all eleven initial
references, citation-chain the relevant studies, and compare their disease,
transport, target, FcRn, expression, and validation design. If independent IBD
physiology transfer has already been adequately demonstrated, consider a
cross-drug/cross-disease transportability test or a study quantifying when biopsy
composition makes apparent tissue distribution non-identifiable. Those alternatives
also require a prior-art audit and adequate data; neither is asserted novel here.

Do not proceed to a publication novelty claim while this audit remains unresolved.
